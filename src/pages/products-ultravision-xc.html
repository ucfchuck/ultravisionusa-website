UltraVision XC

A six-pound point-of-care scanner with a 13-inch, 2880×1920 pixel touch display, built around a high-end Microsoft Surface Pro running Windows 11. All the imaging of the XS in a tablet you can hold in one hand.

Request an online demo Specifications
Why a tablet

Everything a high-end tablet brings, plus ultrasound

Thunderbolt made it possible to put the scanner on a thin cable to the PC, but tablets lacked the durability a medical device needs until Microsoft put a Thunderbolt interface on the Surface Pro. After it withstood our testing, the XC was born.

  • Bluetooth keyboards and mice, Wi-Fi and a multi-touch screen
  • Forward-facing camera for facial recognition, securing user and patient data
  • The image flips automatically as you change the angle you hold the device, resizing to the space available
  • A rotating hand strap with a desk stand that also attaches the XC to a stand or cart
  • Same clinical software, patient database and DICOM output as the XS
Specifications

UltraVision XC at a glance

Display
13″ multi-touch, 2880×1920 pixels, auto-rotating image
Computer
Microsoft Surface Pro with Intel i7 processor, Windows 11
Interface
Thunderbolt 4 between the tablet and the ultrasound electronics
Weight
About six pounds complete
Transducers
2 to 20 MHz linear, curved linear and phased arrays
Modes
B, M, pulse-wave Doppler, colour flow Doppler, compression elastography, harmonic imaging, photoacoustics, needle visualization
Connectivity
Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, USB, forward camera for sign-in
Mounting
Rotating hand strap, desk stand, cart and stand attachment
Applications
Supported today: musculoskeletal, vascular, endocrinology, phlebology, needle guidance. Being prepared: breast, abdominal, cardiac

Software upgrades, not new hardware

Applications grow with your practice

Because the UltraVision design routes every data path through one large FPGA and a GPU, new applications are software algorithm upgrades. Obstetrics, gynecology and prostate scanning will follow customer demand without replacing the system you bought.

Phlebology practices diagnosing deep vein thrombosis, for example, will find the XC with the L14-4 linear transducer and simultaneous colour flow the most efficient tool for the job.
