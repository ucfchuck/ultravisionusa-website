Research

Built as a research platform first

The UltraVision began life as a research platform. It runs on a Windows PC with a GPU, so it can be implemented on any PC with sufficient resources, and systems have been sold to universities and research institutions around the world.

The platform

Open architecture, full control

We normally supply the research platform with a laptop connected to the console over Thunderbolt, which uploads image and RF data at hundreds of megabytes per second. The minimum recommended host is a seventh-generation Intel i7 with its own GPU and a Thunderbolt interface.

The platform may be purchased with an Application Programming Interface that allows user control of most functions, including transmit control.

  • Complete programmable interface in C# with a C++ wrapper
  • Transmit: +HV, −HV, ground and high impedance per channel, 512 delay and 512 waveform profiles at 6 or 4 ns granularity
  • Eight simultaneous beamformers; RF data per beamformer or per element at 40 or 60 MHz digitization
  • 14-bit channel data, 20-bit beamformed data, 16-bit enveloped acoustic lines
  • Diagnostics with a 64-channel oscilloscope view resolvable to one clock and one amplitude level
  • Transducer characteristics editable in an XML file: pitch, geometry, origin, so a new design scans within minutes
Areas of work

Modes and methods we have developed

Our expertise spans compression and ARFI elastography, diverging-wave imaging, GPU pixel beamforming and photoacoustics, as well as all the modes and methods of attaining excellent B-mode and colour flow imaging.

Compression and ARFI elastography

Compression (strain) elastography tracks how tissue moves under light transducer pressure, in real time across the whole image: hard tissue moves as a block while soft tissue compresses. It is cleared for clinical use on the XS and XC.

Acoustic radiation force impulse (ARFI) modes use a focused push from the transducer itself to displace tissue and then image the response. On the research platform these include push displacement imaging, shear-wave speed imaging (shear-wave elastography, with speed proportional to Young's modulus), viscoelastic imaging of the tissue's time-dependent recovery, and frequency dispersion of shear-wave speed to characterize viscoelastic properties.

U.S. Patent 12,773,182

MC-mode: imaging microcalcifications

Our patented mode finds microcalcifications and calcifications by how they move under ultrasound rather than how strongly they echo. Developed to find the microcalcifications that mammography relies on in breast cancer screening, it has also revealed kidney stones and carotid plaque, and it forms the basis of our breast screening commercialization plan.

Diverging-wave and ultrafast imaging

Instead of forming one focused line per transmit, the array fires an unfocused diverging or plane wave that insonifies the whole field at once, and the image is reconstructed from the returned channel data for every pixel. A handful of steered transmits, coherently compounded, replaces hundreds of focused lines, giving frame rates in the thousands per second for shear-wave tracking, flow imaging and the ARFI modes above.

GPU pixel beamformer

Our pixel beamformer moves beamforming off fixed hardware and onto the GPU, reconstructing every pixel directly from the per-channel RF data. Diverging-wave reconstruction, synthetic aperture and custom apodization become software choices that can change from frame to frame, and the result is the best image quality the platform can deliver. It is running today on the latest NVIDIA GPUs, with upgrades continuing while it goes through verification and validation ahead of clinical release.

On the roadmap

High-frequency system

At the request of an existing customer we designed a high-frequency scanner. The analog-to-digital converters were raised to a 16-channel device running up to 125 MHz, and the pulsers to a 16-channel part driving 4 A into each channel to achieve the slew rate that transducer and cable capacitance demand. It fits the customer's familiar layout as a three-board set: two 32-channel mezzanine boards on the FPGA board.

Photoacoustics and custom sequences

Photoacoustic imaging is supported in the clinical reference program alongside harmonic imaging and compression elastography. Researchers specify pulser phase, frequency and delays for custom algorithms, and once an algorithm is proven on the CPU and GPU the UltraVision engineers can instantiate its high-speed components in the FPGA for real-time operation.
