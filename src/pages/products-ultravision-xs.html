UltraVision XS

Designed for the bedside, the private office and the interventional environment. The XS gives the physician enhanced visualization at the point of care for anesthesia, breast, musculoskeletal and urgent care applications.

Features

Image quality and workflow, by design

Proprietary algorithms create uncompromised image quality, and practical features shorten procedure time.

UltraBeam compounding

Multiple scan angles create a single image with enhanced contrast resolution. Simultaneous acoustic lines increase the resolution of small structures and sharpen border delineation.

Needle visualization In development

An advanced algorithm sends acoustic lines to intersect the needle and uses elastography to show the tissue it moves, for precise placement without losing the image around it.

This feature is currently in clinical development and limited by United States law to investigational use (21 CFR 812).

Strain elastography

Non-invasive assessment of tissue stiffness in response to transducer pressure. Valuable for assessing change in tendons, ligaments and nodules.

UltraConnect

A Wi-Fi dongle in a USB port connects the system for upgrades, service, support, training and image optimization, wherever it is.

UltraZoom and UltraView

Pinch to zoom down to less than a square centimetre, about 30 times normal. View up to four images at once in duplex or triplex modes.

One-touch optimization

A single key instantly optimizes the image in B, colour and PW modes. Presets programmed by our consultant sonographers deliver excellent images minutes out of the box.

Inside the XS

Three processors, one programmable design

The power of the UltraVision comes from three processors in ascending order of speed: an Intel i7, a graphics processing unit and a very large field-programmable gate array with several billion transistors.

Engineers use the FPGA architecture to create wide data paths for pipelining and parallel processing, giving up to eight simultaneous beamformers. Faster frame rates come with lower transmit rates, which reduces the patient's exposure to ultrasound energy. Because every data path passes through that one FPGA, upgrades and new functionality are delivered with software alone.

The GPU, programmed in DirectX, creates the high-resolution images in real time. The Windows PC runs the touch interface and brings the internet with it: teleradiology, secure patient information handling and the ability to reprogram and upgrade every device remotely.
